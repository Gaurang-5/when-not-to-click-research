# Submission review of When Not to Click

Reviewed 3 November 2026 for Applied Artificial Intelligence, Taylor & Francis.

Recommendation: revise before submission. The paper has a defensible empirical result and fits the journal's interest in evaluating AI systems, but the current file has substantive reporting gaps and unfinished submission material. This is an author-side review, not an official journal peer review or a prediction of acceptance.

I reviewed all eight pages of conversation history returned for “Evaluate Jev GUI grounding safety,” the supplied DOCX, its 24-page rendered layout, relevant saved analysis code and annotation records, and selected primary references. I recalculated policy counts and checked calibration thresholds offline. I did not alter the manuscript, relabel cases, run paid inference, or rerun the entire research pipeline. Page numbers below refer to the review render; Word pagination can differ.

The later draft has already addressed several earlier criticisms: it contains a matched-input Qwen baseline, five Jev passes, prompt variants, label and geometry sensitivity, Brier skill, ranking metrics, and real-data recalibration. Recommending these as entirely missing would be incorrect. The 100-case independent audit and fresh 180-task human annotation remain incomplete in both the manuscript and the saved verification record.

1. **Resolve the publisher's AI-drafting restriction before submitting.** Taylor & Francis's current author guidance distinguishes permitted assistance from AI creation of initial manuscript drafts, which it disallows. The project history includes substantive manuscript drafting, beyond copyediting. Discuss the actual workflow with the corresponding author/supervisor and obtain editorial clarification about eligibility and remediation. An accurate disclosure is required, but should not be assumed to resolve the drafting restriction. Preserve the history and do not relabel drafting as language polishing. [Publisher guidance](https://authorservices.taylorandfrancis.com/editorial-policies/using-ai-in-your-research-and-manuscript-preparations/); [AI policy](https://taylorandfrancis.com/our-policies/ai-policy/).

2. **Explain AI-assisted annotation corrections and distinguish them from independent initial annotation.** Pages 3–4 describe independent annotations and corrected exports, but omit the nature of correction assistance. The chat contains suggestions to change substantive status labels and draw or retain particular target boxes, including the “share” and “reset password” cases. Comparing archived raw and corrected exports found two changed A annotations and eleven changed B annotations, including eight B status changes. Seven of those status changes are real-interface cases; one is synthetic. These are not all formatting-only changes. This does not establish exposure to Jev outputs or invalidate all labels, but it does require accurate provenance.

   Report the original independent status agreement, 755/951 (79.4%), κ = 0.605, separately from the post-correction agreement, 759/951 (79.8%), κ = 0.613. Identify which decisions received AI assistance and which human verified them. Describe the reviewer-approved B resolution separately from independent third adjudication. Recheck the affected cases through a blinded human process and preserve any new labels as a new sensitivity version.

3. **Qualify the claim that no threshold meets 10% empirical risk.** The abstract, page 8, and Appendix C.3 can be read as a claim about all thresholds. The implementation searches only 0, 0.05, …, 1 for both the empirical comparator and LTT. An offline check of the actual calibration score breakpoints found that original E at 0.7378364638 and real-fitted E at 0.2965903830 each accept one correct calibration case, with zero observed errors. Neither breakpoint is on the tested grid. Therefore, the recorded zero-accept recovery result is valid for the specified grid, but an unrestricted “no threshold” statement is false. Report the grid restriction and the tiny off-grid empirical operating points as a separate retrospective diagnostic. One correct acceptance is not evidence of dependable low-risk automation; do not use test labels to choose a new operating threshold.

4. **Explain why the LTT negative result is structurally underpowered.** Appendix C.3 correctly derives a minimum of 75 error-free accepts for the stated 126-test Bonferroni family, α = 0.10 and δ = 0.05. The entire 226-case calibration split contains only nine strictly correct predictions. Selecting one case per source cluster cannot increase this count. Even an oracle gate could not satisfy this certification procedure on the available calibration outcomes. Thus failure to certify is expected from the available information and cannot isolate failure of recalibration. The empirical-grid failure and the statistical-certification failure need separate interpretations. A future study should plan its calibration sample around the number of correct, accepted cases required, rather than only total dataset size.

5. **Complete the submission material.** Page 13 contains unresolved author-role, conflict, funding, ORCID, AI-use, ethics/consent, and repository/DOI placeholders. The title lists three authors, while CRediT has only two placeholder authors. Affiliations, corresponding-author contact details, and keywords are absent. Page 14 contains five “[FULL AUTHOR LIST]” markers. Confirm declarations with the authors; do not invent an ethics approval, exemption, funding statement, or contribution. Supply a usable repository/archive link and a clear access route for restricted material before calling the work publicly reproducible.

6. **Remove editing instructions and finish figures.** Page 14 still prints “Old → new numbering…”. Table C6 on page 23 retains “Restructured from long to wide format; values unchanged.” Figure 7 on page 11 prints “interval: see artifact” for two comparisons. Replace those notes with the actual intervals or a precise accessible supplementary-table reference. Its headline “Jev beats quantized Qwen…” is stronger and less neutral than the body discussion; identify the tested deployments and cohort in the figure itself. Several tables split awkwardly: Table 4 leaves E alone on the next page, Table A1 splits after two rows, and Table A3 splits a stratum label. On page 16, some coverage/risk entries nearly touch. Adjust widths and row pagination, and inspect at final submission size.

7. **Tighten the operational meaning of an incorrect click.** The primary outcome requires both centre-in-reference-box and IoU ≥ 0.5. A click can fall within the correct control while its predicted box fails IoU. The paper already reports the important 31.3% strict versus 58.8% centre-only difference. Use “failure under the strict localization criterion” where “incorrect click” would imply the click point itself is wrong. Justify strict box overlap for the intended application and give centre-only selective risk comparable prominence. The primary strict analysis can stay frozen; clarify its meaning rather than switching endpoints after seeing results.

8. **Keep the 6.1% bound explicitly tied to this cohort.** The algebra is correct: 41/(751 × 0.9) ≈ 6.07%. It bounds acceptance for the recorded predictions, labels, and strict correctness rule. It also depends on the negative-heavy case mixture. It is not a universal Jev coverage limit or the coverage expected in a natural user workload. The body states this well, but the abstract's shorter wording should retain these qualifications. The real cohort contains 751 cases on 730 source images, not 751 mutually independent observations; “pilot-disjoint real-interface cases” is more precise than an unqualified “independent cases.”

9. **Make the real-only population central throughout.** The real cohort has 583 absent cases, 131 unique cases, and only 29 multiple cases. Unique cases were recovered from source-proposed negative instructions. This is a useful stress cohort, but it is not a representative sample of ordinary GUI tasks or the standard ScreenSpot-v2 positive benchmark. Several appendix strata mix the 200 reused synthetic cases back in. Retain those for audit sensitivity, but provide real-only target-size, confidence, and label-provenance breakdowns prominently. Complete the fresh cohort if feasible, after human labeling and a frozen plan; its 60 ScreenSpot-Pro screenshots form a professional-desktop cohort, not broad platform coverage.

10. **Prioritize independent human verification over simply adding more model APIs.** Annotator choice changes the real unique count from 131 under B to 200 under A, and Table C8 shows corresponding accuracy differences. The prepared 100-case audit is valuable evidence that is still missing. Use it to inspect both disputed and agreed cases, without revealing model results. Its 60 flagged/40 consensus allocation is stratified: report stratum-specific results or weight aggregate estimates appropriately. Do not present an unweighted audit error rate as representative of all 751 cases. Completion is a scientific recommendation, not a claim that the publisher requires a third annotator.

11. **Strengthen the comparison relevant to the paper's contribution.** The matched-input Qwen experiment is a meaningful improvement over the earlier single-model draft. It remains a comparison between a quantized, forced-box local deployment and a native hosted API. Preserve the caveats around quantization, coordinate prompting, structured decoding, and hardware. Additional grounded evidence could come from a GUI-specialized baseline or a comparable uncertainty/consistency method, assessed on the same human-verified holdout. The related-work section should explain how this audit differs from SafeGround and Argus, rather than merely listing them. Their primary sources already address uncertainty and transfer across GUI settings. [SafeGround](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02419); [Argus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25760).

12. **Expose omitted ablations and historical hypothesis outcomes.** Table 1 defines A, presence-only, and presence-plus-uniqueness, but the main results omit them. Recalculated real-cohort counts are A: 740 accepts/699 errors; presence-only: 1/0; presence-plus-uniqueness: 0/0. Presence-only therefore achieves the same headline acceptance/error counts as E here. Include a complete ablation table, even if supplementary, to make the complexity comparison clear. The original pilot report also states that its prospective practical criterion failed because the box baseline already had zero absent-target accepts. Briefly report that outcome and distinguish the later exploratory emphasis from the initial criterion.

13. **Make the experiment reproducible from the submitted package.** Prompt IDs and statements that hashes were recorded are useful but insufficient without accessible artifacts. Include the exact Jev prompt/schema, both prompt variants, Qwen prompt/schema, model digest, software versions, parser tolerances, threshold candidate construction, regression regularization/settings, and complete-precision coefficients. The actual preprocessing downsizes to a maximum 1,568-pixel side, pads to a minimum 280 pixels per side, and saves JPEG at quality 92; these choices and coordinate transformations should be documented. State collection dates and clarify which quantities use 751 real cases, 740 valid real outputs, 131 unique targets, or 951 mixed inputs. The general 2,000-bootstrap description should explicitly note Table C4's 1,000-bootstrap exception.

14. **Refine statistical comparisons.** Overlapping AUROC intervals do not establish equality or lack of added value. The existing cautious conclusion is reasonable, but a paired source-cluster interval for E minus B would directly assess incremental discrimination. Reliability figures should show bin counts and uncertainty, because sparsely occupied bins can look persuasive. Define all six target states and the evidence required to distinguish absent, offscreen, occluded, and unclear targets from a static screenshot. Explain any priority rule when states overlap.

15. **Reduce material that distracts from the main finding.** The utility formulas and pairwise break-even values are correct, but lengthy textbook decision theory and historical GPT-4o pricing add less than verified annotation and transfer evidence. Consider shortening Appendix D and the main utility discussion. The abstract is 175 whitespace-delimited words, so a blanket claim that it is excessively long would be wrong; it is numerically dense and could better emphasize one main result. The title is relevant but long. The strongest contribution remains the empirical separation of localization limits, confidence transfer, and cohort provenance.

16. **Complete reference and artifact checking.** The selected newer references resolve to the stated papers, and the GoClick Table 3 values reproduced in Appendix D match the source. The Argus record now identifies NeurIPS 2026 acceptance, so update its bibliographic status as appropriate. Finish author lists and verify every citation against its primary record. Check source-image permissions for published qualitative figures as well as for dataset redistribution. The data-availability statement should distinguish locally retained evidence, material available to reviewers, publicly downloadable code, and restricted images/labels.

The numerical core checked in this review is consistent: Table 3 policy counts, 16/26 = 61.5% D risk, 41/131 = 31.3% strict localization, the Brier constant 0.05234 and E skill approximately −0.170, the finite-cohort coverage bound, and the utility break-even points. The principal issues are provenance, interpretation, reproducibility, and submission readiness, rather than widespread arithmetic errors.

The journal's official scope includes evaluation of existing AI tools and comparative studies, so an audit paper can be suitable without inventing a new algorithm. Nevertheless, a narrow negative result needs strong labels, accessible evidence, and a clear practical lesson. I verified the scope through the official indexed page. The journal-specific Instructions for Authors page returned HTTP 403, so I have not certified its current article-type length, abstract limit, anonymization, or file-format requirements. Do not infer those requirements from a published article's appearance. [Official scope](https://www.tandfonline.com/action/journalInformation?journalCode=uaai20&show=aimsScope); [Instructions for Authors](https://www.tandfonline.com/action/authorSubmission?show=instructions&journalCode=uaai20).

Recommended order: resolve the publisher-policy question and annotation provenance; correct the threshold and LTT interpretation; complete independent review and, if feasible, fresh-cohort validation; finish the artifacts and declarations; then perform the final reference and layout check. More paid inference is not the first priority.
