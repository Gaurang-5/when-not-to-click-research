Before a graphical user interface (GUI) agent clicks, it must check that the requested control is present. It must also identify that control uniquely. We test frozen selective-execution policies using the native outputs of a pinned visual interface, Jev 1.14. For each request, it returns a visible-presence probability, a match-count distribution and one bounding box. Presence and uniqueness gates made no incorrect accepted clicks on held-out synthetic layouts. The result did not carry over to real interfaces. A source audit found that 200 of the 951 human-labelled cases reused pilot screenshots. Among the 751 independent real-interface cases, the three-signal policy accepted 26 with a conditional risk of 61.5%; the frozen logistic gate accepted one. Strict localization succeeded on 41 of 131 unique targets (31.3%), limiting any gate to about 6.1% coverage at 10% accepted-error risk. Recalibration on real data improved Brier loss, but no threshold met the 10% risk target. A quantized Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline tested on matched inputs achieved 16.8% strict accuracy. Our audit documents the transfer failure and cohort provenance. It also shows how the denominators affect the interpretation of safety metrics. We do not introduce a new uncertainty-control algorithm.

Probability quality. Original E has a Brier loss of 0.06125, compared with 0.05234 for the retrospective constant-prevalence predictor. E therefore has a Brier skill of −0.170 (Equation C1). Its low expected calibration error (ECE) does not, on its own, show that the probabilities are useful. The extra native signals also add little discrimination. On the real cohort, B and E have nearly the same AUROC, and their source-cluster intervals overlap. The full ranking and Brier results appear in Appendix C.2.

Native presence and uniqueness signals substantially improve selective clicking on authored synthetic layouts. We do not find comparable evidence of usefulness on real interfaces. After removing the 200 reused pilot cases, the frozen logistic gate accepts just one of 751 real cases. The three-signal policy still has a conditional risk of 61.5%. The study documents this failure to transfer, traces the cohort provenance and shows how denominators affect the interpretation of safety metrics. In tests with the same interface, the quantized Qwen baseline has lower strict localization accuracy. For both models, acceptance remains unreliable under the semantic one-label rule. These results do not establish broader model superiority, formal safety or practical utility.

They exclude cases in which the annotators disagree on status, and there are no independently verified true boxes. They therefore set no absolute ceiling on human or model performance.
